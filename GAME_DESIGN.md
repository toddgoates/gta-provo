# Game Design · Provo

## Vision

A third-person neighborhood sandbox in a compressed, fictional Utah Valley. Take the readable streets and quick mission pacing of early PS2 open worlds; give them original characters and a downtown where a dispute over a folding chair can become municipal business.

## Pillars

- **Small city, big opinions.** Satire comes from local rituals, neighborhood politics, hobbyhorses, and wildly disproportionate civic stakes.
- **Learn the streets by playing.** Repeated visits, recognizable storefronts, and the mountains should orient the player before a map is needed.
- **Keep it quick and physical.** Responsive walking now; approachable arcade driving and short optional errands later. Favor short, replayable interactions over simulation for its own sake.
- **Make constraints visible.** Chunky silhouettes, flat shading, painted signs, modest draw distance, and warm storefront colors against cool mountain layers.

## First playable: “A Minor Civic Emergency”

Start at Center & University near the Soda Dept. Talk to Marla Jensen, choose a reply, and confirm the committee’s folding chair at the Welcome Pavilion by reaching the marker. Marla pays $15. The neighborhood stays open to explore; nothing else unlocks in this build.

The playable grid is three built blocks plus a public plaza. One static NPC, a few text lines, and one walking route are sufficient to validate the loop. Light NPC traffic makes crossing University Avenue feel like crossing a street: compact cars, sedans, and pickups follow their lanes and take turns at the four-way stop.

## Deferred

Broader districts and neighborhood streaming, interiors, roaming pedestrians, turning between streets and larger traffic networks, vehicle damage and crash reactions, optional activities, a campaign, local saves, GLB art, voice acting, radio/music and ambience, day/night, and multi-step cutscenes. No external generation APIs are required to run the game.

**Next milestone:** Tune arcade handling and build a short driving objective on the existing streets before growing the map.
